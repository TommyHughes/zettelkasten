# Arguments

## The Hard Problem

[source](https://youtu.be/p5ZevvpijP4?si=OOWlf5sM577vdQGk&t=1004)

> INTERVIEWER: Aren't all the examples you just mentioned processes that science is explaining?
>
> ROVELLI: Yeah
>
> INTERVIEWER: It doesn't explain the mystery of how it feels to be an individual.
>
> ROVELLI: No. Why shouldn't it be able to explain that? I don't see the incomprehensible part of it. I see many open questions, but not something which is simply impossible to address with standard ways.

Boomerism.

Tell us then, if it's not impossible, then explain how it would be possible. What would a explanation even look like? It doesn't have to be correct. The physics could be wildly wildly incorrect. All we're looking for is a possible explanation for what consciousness could be in purely physical terms that doesn't just posit that they don't exist or are just something else.

Science investigates physical reality and it is for precisely this reason that science, as it exists today, will never provide an adequate response to the interviewer's question. This is because the current notion of *physical* is not yet adequate to explain conscious phenomena. There is nothing for experimenters or theoreticians to go looking for at the moment or anything we can even imagine that we would find that would even begin to build a space for the solution in current physical terms.

We can even relax the criteria immensely and simply ask which physical characteristics and dynamics consciousness could conceivably be built from: disregard the details or correctness and just give us a physical system from which we can build consciousness qua consciousness; **The color purple is nothing but this physical system**.

> INTERVIEWER: So let's say you have a neuroscientist and they are studying the brain, mapping every possible brain state using all the latest technology. But at the end of the day, all they've done is study the brain, and not what it feels like to be a human.
>
> ROVELLI: Why? How do you know?

Because all the scientist will be able to tell me is a story about my nervous systems and the physical matter therein, not about feelings or thoughts.

## Happy Fishes

[source](https://youtu.be/p5ZevvpijP4?si=bQua18pSk7C5ypzb&t=1177)

> "You knew what I was thinking...the fact that we are communicating shows you know something about my own mind...there is no sphere of reality which is inaccessible."

>"It's an illusion. There is no ghost in the machine. There is no sharp separation in the universe between different levels of reality."

First of all, the argument is just false, because the speaker in the story did **not** "know" what he was thinking. Even when confirmed, no one **knew** what anyone else was thinking except possibly only what they themselves were thinking. 

I think the latter quote answers this though. Rovelli says we never have complete information about anything so the notion of knowing or certainty is irrelevant.

The major problem, of course, is that this has almost nothing to do with the hard problem of consciousness. The hard problem is asking the question about how inner experience is possible at all from a physicalist ontology.
