# The Education of <Name>

A coming-of-age biographically-styled story of a boy growing up in a society where education covers all facets of human life.

Parts of the education emphasize academic learning (math, science, history, reading, writing, literature, medicine, etc.)
, other parts emphasize practical knowledge (construction, cooking, collective decision making, home-making, child-rearing, ...)
, others emphasize spiritual and social knowledge (religion, relationships, therapy, leadership, elder-care, ...)
, other parts emphasize ...

The idea is that it is a realist utopian picture of society. It should be told in such a way that it's not focused on the societal/political elements of the world but on the wonder and human elements of childhood and growing up.

There may be room for the idea that some of this work is dangerous. While harm-reduction is a priority in the society, there is a tolerance for loss and peril within education that permits students to enter into dangerous situations on their own, and where real harm can come to them during their studies. This is not an emphasis in the book.

The story should proceed as though all the strange differences between the world of the story and the real world as just assumed normal, or with natural skepticism that anybody might have entering in those situations. It should play more of a backdrop than any sort of thematic or philosophical part.

There is a sense in which there should be no themes or symbolism or political messages in the book. It is just a story about childhood. It should focus on the emotions and feelings associated with growing up.
