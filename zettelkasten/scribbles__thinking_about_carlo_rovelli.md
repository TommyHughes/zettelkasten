# Thinking about Carlo Rovelli

I recently watched an interview Carlo Rovelli did with *The New Society* and I want to share some thoughts about his philosophy.

I sort of want Rovelli to speak for himself here, but I can describe his ideas in brief before we get started. Rovelli is a 
physicist who argues for an anti-foundationalist view of the world. He thinks that there is no fundamental nature of reality and that we should view the world as nothing more than a system of relations. He is the founder of Relational Quantum Mechanics which is an interpretation of quantum mechanics that maintains that there is no such thing as an observer-independent state of a system. He explicitly describes this interpretation as purely physical.

Over the years, he's been quite critical, disparaging even, of the mind-body problem. He routinely asserts that ["there is no hard problem of consciousness"](https://www.noemamag.com/there-is-no-hard-problem-of-consciousness/).

I think this is as close as Rovelli gets in this interview to actually addressing the mind-body problem directly. From his response I take two basic ideas:

1. The hard problem of consciousness is analgous to the "elan vital" of vitalism.

1. Consciousness, or "soul" as Rovelli puts it, is explainable with "standard ways". That is, it is not "incomprehensible" that consciousness can be explained by existing physics.
