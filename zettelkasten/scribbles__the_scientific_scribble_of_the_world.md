| 9     |          | For example, let's look at the Physicist's "dynamical law". These are the so-called laws of motion: Newtonian, Schrödinger Equation, Least Action.<br><br>But what *is* a law really? Suppose I kick a rock. It tumbles. Why? Why does it move at all? If it does move, why does it move in the specific way that it does?<br><br>What Physics gives me is a "dynamical law"; a principle which characterizes the movements of the rock as it tumbles. The explanation might start with a report about "forces" and end up with a conversation about least action, information axioms, or wave functions.<br><br>But what all these explanations do is characterize the way the rock moves in such detail that they generalize to principles that characterize which trajectory or path all changes satisfy; they describe the characteristics of the way the world changes and how it does it.<br><br>But my question is why that one and not another. If Physics gives me a dynamical law, I'd ask "why this law" or better "why a law at all"? Even further "What must be true for law-like behavior to even be possible" and then why are those?                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                  |           |
| 10    |          | Even if we postpone our questioning about why a given law holds and not another, and accept the laws that Physics gives us, I might still ask "how should this law be understood?"<br><br>For example, let's go back to my rock. When we inspect the relevant physics, we find no rock laws. There are no laws of physics that describe how rocks behave. Instead we get a relatively compact collection of formulae and principles which we assume the rock, and all things, “obey”.<br><br>There’s an assumed universality and reductionism baked into these laws. They describe how particles or fields or some other physical variables “behave”, but no rock formulas. The rock is an assemblage of these more basic compressed laws that know nothing, in themselves, about rocks. Indeed, it is by these laws that rocks even come to be.<br><br>But how do we know that? The physicist will write down some very tidy formulas that apply broadly, but what is the physical principle that guarantees this is actually what is happening; this is what things are doing? Why couldn’t it be the case that what the world is actually doing is following a kind of case-by-case rule, where each happening is a unique, non-homogeneous, and non-universal. That is, there is no electromagnetism, there is no gravity, there are no relations, no cause and effect, there is only the action itself that implies nothing about anything else. |           |
| 11    |          | Don’t get me wrong I don’t expect the Physicist to have an answer. Physics would become nothing but a set of useful fictions that express nothing about what is real.<br><br>A Physicist might even ask “what would be the difference? If all the behavior is the same, then these two descriptions are equivalent.”<br><br>And I would applaud such a question. I think it’s a good first step towards realizing the limits of the empirical enterprise<br><br>A lot of physicists these days seem to be interested in a kind of philosophical activism. For them, there is a need for physics to not *merely* be useful, but “true”. So much so that they’d have us believe that Physics is all that there is, that there is no metaphysics. That all there is to reality, are these physical laws or a system of physical relations. That there is no such thing as “magic” or “soul” or rather that the soul just is these physical laws. That there is no “what it is”, only “what it does”.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |           |
